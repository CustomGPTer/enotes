Privacy policy

Last updated 22 November 2026. This policy covers the ENotes app for Android (package uk.jj.enotes) and this website. ENotes is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom.

The short version

  • ENotes does not have Android's internet permission. It cannot send anything from your phone, to the developer or to anyone else.
  • ENotes has no accounts and no servers. The developer receives nothing from your phone and cannot see, open or recover your notes.
  • There is no advertising and no analytics in the app.
  • Everything ENotes keeps is on your phone, encrypted, in storage that other apps cannot read. It is deleted when you uninstall the app.

What stays on your phone

ENotes keeps the following in its private storage, encrypted:

  • Your notes, with their titles, text, tags, colours and folders.
  • Photos, scans, drawings, recordings, PDFs and other files you add to notes.
  • Earlier versions of your notes, your own templates and your recent searches.
  • The key that opens your notes, stored only in locked form: locked with your password, with your recovery code, and, if you turn it on, with a key released by your fingerprint. Your password and recovery code are never stored.

Two things are kept unencrypted, because Android needs them while ENotes is locked: your settings (such as the theme and the auto-lock time), and for each reminder its time, whether it repeats and a random number that points to the note. The note itself, and what it says, stay encrypted.

None of this is included in Android's backup or in a transfer to a new phone. The only copies that leave the app are a backup file or a note you share, and only when you make one and choose where it goes.

ENotes does not ask for the internet, your contacts, your location, your messages or access to all your files.

Services the app contacts

None. ENotes has no internet permission and contacts nothing. Copy the updates link in Settings copies the address of the download page on GitHub; if you open it in your browser, what happens then is between your browser and GitHub, as described under This website.

Other people

Your notes, photos and recordings may be about other people. They stay on your phone and are yours to manage, and you are responsible for how you use them.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • A note you delete goes to the recycle bin for 30 days and is then removed with its attachments and earlier versions. You can empty the bin at any time.
  • Settings › Delete everything destroys the keys and then every note.
  • Clearing the app's storage or uninstalling ENotes deletes everything it holds.
  • A backup file, or a note you have shared or exported, is an ordinary file wherever you saved or sent it. Delete it there when you no longer want it.
  • Nothing is held anywhere else, so there is nothing further to delete.

Your rights

UK and EU data protection law gives you rights over personal data that an organisation holds about you, including the right to see it, correct it and have it deleted.

The developer of ENotes holds no personal data about you: there is no account, no server and no log of who uses the app. What ENotes stores is on your own phone, where you control it directly using the steps above. If you need help, use the contact link below. You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Security

Notes and attachments are encrypted with AES-256-GCM, using keys made for each kind of data from one main key. Your password is stretched with Argon2id and is never stored. The main key is also locked with a key held in the phone's security hardware. Because ENotes cannot go online, nothing it holds can be taken over a network through the app.

No software is free of faults, and ENotes has not had an independent security review. It cannot protect open notes from software with full control of a rooted phone.

This website

This site sets no cookies and has no analytics or advertising. It is hosted by GitHub Pages, and the app file is downloaded from GitHub Releases. GitHub, as the host, receives the technical details any web server sees, such as your IP address and browser type, and handles them under the GitHub privacy statement. The developer does not receive them.

Children

ENotes is not aimed at children and collects nothing from anyone. A parent or guardian should decide whether a child uses it.

Contact

Questions about this policy can be raised on GitHub. This policy was last updated on 22 November 2026.
